We were broadcasting live from Detroit when a good-looking, forty-two-year-old woman stopped by to say hello. A man kept leaning in close, and I finally asked her, “What does he want?” She laughed: “Just small talk.” So I told her to look him in the eye and tell him how many kids she had. “Five,” she said. He jumped back like a spooked horse, and by the next commercial break he was fifty feet away, in some other woman’s face.

Introduction: A Peek Inside the Male Mind

Steve Harvey is a comedian and radio host, and for years women have written to his show asking one question: why do men do what they do? His book answers from the inside, from a man who has played the field, blown it, and finally gotten it right. The title is the whole idea: think like a man so you can understand him, and act like a lady so you get treated like one.

A note on perspective

This is one man’s opinion, aimed at women dating men. Much of it is generalization, so treat it as a point of view, not a law that fits everybody.

Takeaway: Before you judge a partner or a prospect, understand what he needs and wants, and then decide what you need and want.

Part 1: The Mindset of a Man

Chapter 1: What Drives Men

Men are simple, Harvey says. We are driven by three things: who we are, what we do, and how much we make. Until those three line up, a woman fits into the cracks of his life. He isn’t thinking about settling down, kids, or a home. He doesn’t have to have made it, but he has to be on the way.

Harvey lived it. After he was laid off from Ford, he bounced through one job after another until he won a fifty-dollar amateur night. He spent fifteen of those dollars on business cards: “Steve Harvey. Comedian.” Who he was and what he did were set, and that was enough to get him moving.

Takeaway: Ask the man you’re dating: what’s your dream, and what step are you taking toward it right now?

Chapter 2: Our Love Isn’t Like Your Love

A woman’s love is patient and unconditional, and she expects a man’s to be the same. It isn’t. A man’s love is simpler and more direct. He isn’t going to call every half hour. But he will do three things if he loves you.

Profess→Provide→Protect

He will profess you: “this is my woman,” not just “my friend.” He will provide for you, with money or with sweat, however he can. And he will protect you and your family, sometimes more than you’d like. If he does none of the three, Harvey says, he hasn’t fallen in love with you yet.

Takeaway: Look for action, not talk. Does he claim you in public, provide, and protect?

Chapter 3: Support, Loyalty, and the Cookie

Beyond those, Harvey names three things a man needs from a woman: support, loyalty, and “the cookie,” his word for physical intimacy. A man wants to feel needed, and the easiest way to give him that is to let him provide for you and back his dream.

Takeaway: Give support and loyalty to a man who has earned it, not up front.

Chapter 4: “We Need to Talk,” and Other Words That Make Men Run

To a man, “we need to talk” means one of two things: he did something wrong, or, worse, you really just want to talk. Women talk to share. Men talk to fix. Say a coworker wore the same blouse as you. Your girlfriend will commiserate for hours. A man will say, “Don’t wear it anymore.” Done.

Where it comes from

Boys are told to stop crying and “be a man.” So don’t expect a grown man to suddenly sit and listen without trying to repair it.

Takeaway: Say what you mean plainly and directly, or take the venting to your girls, not to a man who just wants to fix it.

Part 2: Why Men Do What They Do

Chapter 5: First Things First, He Wants to Sleep with You

Harvey is blunt: when a man walks up to you, what he sees is how you look, not your job, your car, or your kids. In that Detroit story, the guy bolted the second he heard “five kids,” because five kids was not part of his plan, and his plan was one thing.

Takeaway: Don’t read intentions from sweet talk. Watch whether he asks about your life and cares about the answers.

Chapter 6: Sports Fish vs. Keepers

Harvey loves to fish, and he uses it as a metaphor. A man fishing for sport catches the biggest fish he can, takes a picture, and throws it back. A man fishing to eat takes it home. A woman with no rules, requirements, or plans reads from a mile away as a sports fish. A woman with standards gets treated as a keeper.

Sports fishNever gets introduced to family or friends.

And if he’s only sport fishing, let him walk. What do you care? He’s not the one.

Takeaway: Show early that you have requirements. If that scares him off, he wasn’t your guy.

Chapter 7: Mama’s Boys

A man’s relationship with his mother is his first one with a woman. It’s where he learns to protect and provide, and it tells you a lot about how he’ll treat you. But a man who still lives under his mother’s thumb, Harvey says, isn’t ready to be a partner.

Takeaway: Watch how he treats his mother: respect, yes, but can he still make his own decisions?

Chapter 8: Why Men Cheat

Harvey says plainly he is not justifying cheating, only explaining. First, men often see sex as physical, while many women see it as an act of love. Second, a cheater is usually confident he won’t get caught. Third, some men who haven’t reached the three goals (who he is, what he does, how much he makes) look for validation from other women.

What you can do

Set requirements early, ask the five questions, follow the Ninety-Day Rule, and release yourself from the blame. The cheating is on him, not you.

Takeaway: Say up front that faithfulness is non-negotiable, and be ready to leave if it is broken.

Part 3: The Playbook, Setting Standards

Chapter 9: Men Respect Standards, So Get Some

I’m not trying to be anybody’s plaything or anybody’s woman on a string.Marjorie Harvey, as Steve tells it in this chapter

Harvey met his wife, Marjorie, at a show in Memphis. Years later, on a Valentine’s weekend, he found her in the hallway at 3 a.m. in her fur coat with her suitcase, because a female friend had called his phone. She told him she had kids and a good life and wanted a man who would complete her family. That is when he knew she was the one. Standards that are clear and enforced earn a man’s respect.

Takeaway: Write down your standards, state them calmly, and be willing to enforce them.

Chapter 10: The Five Questions to Ask Before You Get in Too Deep

Harvey asked men who juggle many women how they keep it going. The answer was the same every time: “They don’t know where it’s going because they never ask.” So ask these five early:

1. Short-term planWhat are his goals now, and what is he doing about them?
2. Long-term planWhere does he see himself in ten years?
3. Family and faithKids, mother, father, and God.
4. What he thinks of youPress for specifics, not generic praise.
5. What he feels for you“Think” and “feel” are not the same.
The ruleIf he has no plan, move on.
Takeaway: Don’t fear losing him by asking. If the questions make him run, you have your answer.

Chapter 11: The Ninety-Day Rule

He. Can. Wait.Steve Harvey, on men and sex

Harvey compares it to his job at Ford: you didn’t get health benefits until you had put in ninety days. So why hand out “benefits” to a man before he has proven himself? Wait ninety days for intimacy, and use that time to test him: does he show up when he says, call when he’s late, like your friends and kids, and does anything seem hidden?

Days 1–90: watch and test→Pass?→Then give the benefits
Takeaway: Let time show who he is. A man chasing only one thing has trouble faking it for three months.

Part 3, Continued: From the Kids to the Ring

Chapter 12: If He Meets the Kids After You Decide He’s the One, It’s Too Late

A man chasing one thing doesn’t want to meet your kids. Most single moms keep a new man away from home, but your children can be the best test. Good signs: he wants to meet them, brings them gifts, respects you in front of them, and is comfortable around kids. A man who vanishes or looks uncomfortable around them deserves a second look.

Takeaway: Introduce the kids after he passes the early tests, but before you’re too far gone.

Chapter 13: Strong, Independent, and Lonely Women

Harvey salutes modern women: working full time, raising kids, running offices and churches. But he argues that a woman who always takes charge leaves a man with no role, and men need to feel needed. His advice is to loosen your grip now and then, ask for help, and show appreciation.

Takeaway: Make room for your partner to step up, and say thank you when he does.

Chapter 14: How to Get the Ring

Plenty of women wait for a proposal that never comes while doing everything a wife would do. A man commits, Harvey says, because he doesn’t want to lose you. After that, tell him you want marriage, ask him to set a date, and give him a deadline. He says this isn’t proposing to him yourself. It’s refusing to wait around like it’s 1945. Don’t be Baltic Avenue on the Monopoly board; be Broadway.

Takeaway: Set a reasonable deadline, and be prepared to act if it passes.

Chapter 15: Quick Answers to Questions You’ve Always Wanted to Ask

The last chapter is a rapid-fire Q&A from Harvey’s female colleagues. The gist: every man has different taste, so what you see as a flaw may be exactly what someone else loves. The rest follows the book’s core: be honest, know what you want, and don’t trade it for outside pressure.

Takeaway: Men differ. Don’t turn one bad experience into a law about all of them.

Three Questions to Sit With

  1. Which standards do I hold, and which do I ignore just because I fear losing someone?
  2. When did I last ask someone plainly about his plans and intentions?
  3. Am I judging a person by months of actions, or by sweet talk in the first weeks?

Closing: Respect Yourself, Understand Him

The book’s message is simple: men are easier to read than you think, and you have more power than you think. You don’t have to turn into someone else. You have to know what you want, say it early, and let his actions answer.

Remember too that this is one point of view. Take what helps, test it against your own experience, and keep trusting your own judgment.
